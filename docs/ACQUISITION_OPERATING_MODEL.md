# Energy Dial Acquisition Operating Model

## Mission

Win the first $997 monthly client without pretending Energy Dial has proof it does not yet have. Build proof through private, technically credible samples; sell a narrow recurring outcome; deliver a first win quickly; then use retained clients to finance authority content, systems, and team capacity.

## Starting Constraints

- Founder time: 25 hours per week.
- Audience, testimonials, and warm network: zero.
- Trial capacity: 10 prospects.
- Current paid offer: four educational clips per month for $997.
- Long-form concept: $8,000, not sold until scope and delivery capacity are proven.
- Differentiation: petroleum-engineering judgment, accurate moment selection, relevant maps/data/diagrams, brand-safe pacing, and technical thumbnails.
- Payment: Flutterwave payment link.
- VSL: deferred until three paying clients.

## The Commercial Thesis

Energy experts already create valuable long-form material. Many fail to distribute the strongest ideas in a form that is understandable, brand-safe, and connected to a commercial offer. Energy Dial does not sell generic editing. It sells a technically credible bridge from owned long-form expertise to repeated discovery and then back to the prospect's real transaction.

## Offer Design

### Founding offer

**Energy Dial Weekly**

- Four 20-60 second educational clips per month.
- One source episode nominated per week.
- Clip selection with a written rationale.
- Relevant maps, labels, arrows, persistent numbers, and factual B-roll.
- One thumbnail direction per clip when needed.
- Platform-specific title/caption/CTA brief.
- 24-hour first-cut target after source confirmation.
- One consolidated revision round.
- Clean select delivered privately.
- Founding price: $997 per month, paid before the first paid production week.

Do not promise four hours of source review per client without measuring it. At 15 clients that alone would consume 60 hours per month before editing. Track source-review minutes for the first ten trials and set the included allowance from actual delivery data.

### Trial

- Unsolicited proof: one private 20-45 second finished sample for only the highest-ranked prospects.
- Accepted trial: two additional clips, for three free clips total.
- Trial expires after seven calendar days or one source episode.
- The trial does not include unlimited revisions, posting, or a strategy retainer.
- Ask for permission to use approved work as a portfolio example; permission is not a condition of the trial.

### Risk reversal

Use a controllable guarantee, not a views guarantee:

> If the first paid clip misses the agreed visual brief, Energy Dial will revise it once at no charge and credit that clip toward the next delivery window.

Do not guarantee reach, leads, or revenue before baseline data and posting control exist.

### Bonuses with independent value

1. **Opportunity Map**: three moments from the client's archive or current episode ranked by relevance, visual potential, and BOF connection.
2. **Posting Pack**: platform title, caption, first comment, source link, thumbnail direction, and CTA for each finished clip.
3. **Evidence Pack**: the maps, public data sources, terminology checks, and visual references used in the edit.
4. **Clean Select Library**: organized clean excerpts the client can reuse privately without diluting the public premium edit.
5. **Monthly Distribution Review**: a short report on what was posted, what held attention, which topics produced qualified responses, and what to test next.

These bonuses remove selection, posting, credibility, and learning friction. They should not be decorative PDFs.

## Price Discipline

- Do not reduce the same four-clip package from $997 to $297 after five rejections. Five objections cannot distinguish bad targeting, weak proof, unclear offer, poor sales execution, or price resistance.
- Keep $997 through at least 30 qualified conversations or five completed trials.
- If a true budget objection repeatedly appears after the prospect values the work, offer a smaller scope, not the same scope at a discount: two clips for $597 or one paid pilot for $350 credited toward the first month.
- Do not display the downsell publicly. Use it only after a qualified prospect wants the outcome but cannot approve $997.
- Raise the founding price after three retained clients or when the close rate exceeds one in three qualified opportunities.

## Capacity Math

At 25 hours per week, the founder has about 108 hours per average month.

Reserve:

- 25 hours for sales, follow-up, and account communication.
- 10 hours for administration and system maintenance.
- 10 hours for Energy Dial authority content.
- 63 hours for fulfillment.

At four clips per client, five clients require 20 clips per month. If source review, edit, QA, revision, export, and posting pack average three hours per clip, five clients consume 60 hours. That is the solo ceiling before the process becomes brittle.

Trigger contractor documentation and testing at three clients. Add editing capacity before client six. Fifteen short-form clients is a team target, not a solo target.

## Lead Math For The First Client

The following is a planning model, not an industry benchmark:

| Stage | Conservative planning count |
| --- | ---: |
| Fit-ranked records inspected | 100 |
| Fully qualified prospects | 40 |
| Personalized first contacts | 30 |
| Replies | 8-10 |
| Trial conversations | 4-5 |
| Completed three-clip trials | 3 |
| Paying monthly clients | 1 |

The first operational target is therefore 30 qualified personalized contacts, not five.

### Diagnostic thresholds

- Fewer than three replies from 30 qualified contacts: fix targeting, subject/opening, or contact channel.
- Replies but fewer than two trial accepts: fix the sample, trial framing, or perceived implementation burden.
- Three completed trials and no close: inspect offer, proof, approval owner, timing, and sales conversation before touching price.
- One close but early churn: fix onboarding, approval speed, posting workflow, and first-seven-day result.
- More demand than can be fulfilled inside the SLA: stop adding trials, document the workflow, and add capacity.

## Weekly Time Budget

### Five normal three-hour days

- 45 minutes: inspect and qualify five fit-ranked prospects.
- 45 minutes: send five personalized DMs/emails or follow-ups.
- 75 minutes: sample or client production.
- 15 minutes: update outcomes and next actions.

### Two five-hour days

- 3 hours: one high-quality private sample or a client delivery block.
- 1 hour: follow-up and sales conversations.
- 1 hour: publish one Energy Dial authority asset or improve the proof page.

Weekly targets:

- 25 new qualified contacts.
- 15 follow-ups.
- Two private samples for top-ranked prospects.
- One Energy Dial authority clip.
- One pipeline review.

## Five Acquisition Plans

### Plan 1: Private Sample Outbound

Best for the first client. Use DialDash to select prospects with current long-form supply, a verified offer, reachable buyer, and weak educational video distribution.

1. Produce one private sample.
2. Send one observation, the sample, and the three-clip trial.
3. Follow up by email after two to three business days.
4. Close asynchronously with scope and Flutterwave; offer a 15-minute call.

Advantages: strongest proof with zero audience. Constraint: slow and production-heavy.

### Plan 2: Audit-First Outbound

Send a short visual-distribution audit before creating a speculative edit.

1. Identify one missed clip moment and one funnel break.
2. Send a 60-90 second screen recording or one-page audit.
3. Make the first finished clip only for interested replies.

Advantages: more contacts per hour. Constraint: weaker initial proof. Use when private-sample reply rates are healthy but sample capacity is the bottleneck.

### Plan 3: Authority Content Inbound

Publish one technically accurate Energy Dial clip per week using public energy content where rights and commentary use are defensible. Teach why the visual treatment improves understanding; do not simply repost another creator's work.

Advantages: compounds trust and supports outbound. Constraint: unlikely to create the first client quickly from a zero audience. Treat it as proof infrastructure, not the primary acquisition engine.

### Plan 4: Producer And Newsletter Partnerships

Approach podcast producers, fractional content teams, energy newsletter operators, and event organizers who already manage several experts.

Offer white-label technical clip selection and visual production. Pay referral fees only after collected revenue and define client ownership in writing.

Advantages: one relationship can create multiple accounts. Constraint: lower margin and less control.

### Plan 5: Focused Hybrid

Recommended 90-day model:

- 70% of acquisition time: Plan 1.
- 20%: authority content that demonstrates technical judgment.
- 10%: partnership conversations.

Do not run five equal strategies. One primary motion produces clean learning; the others support it.

## Outreach Scripts

### First DM with private sample

> [Name] - the point you made in [episode] about [specific idea] would work unusually well as a visual explainer. I made a private 30-second version using [map/diagram/persistent number] so the idea stays technically accurate rather than becoming a rapid-cut clip. Here it is: [link]. I am a petroleum engineer building Energy Dial. If the direction is useful, I will make two more clips from the episode free. If it is not, no problem.

### Email follow-up

Subject: `Private cut from [episode title]`

> [Name] - I sent this on [X/LinkedIn] and wanted to make sure it reached you. I selected the [specific moment] because it connects naturally to [verified offer or audience action]. The private cut is here: [link]. I can make two additional clips free and deliver the motioned versions plus clean selects. No posting commitment required.

### Trial-to-paid close

> Of the three clips, which direction best represents how you want the show to look? If you want this handled weekly, the founding package is four clips per month for $997, including selection rationale, the motioned edit, clean select, thumbnail direction, and posting pack. I can send the one-page scope and Flutterwave link now, or we can use 15 minutes to settle workflow and approvals.

### Price objection

> Understood. Is the constraint the monthly amount, or that the outcome is not yet proven enough to justify it? If it is proof, let us evaluate the three trial clips first. If it is budget after you want the work, I can reduce the scope to two clips rather than reduce the standard of the four-clip package.

## Onboarding And Retention

### First 48 hours

- Personalized welcome message.
- Confirm content owner, approval owner, source-delivery method, platform, CTA, prohibited claims, brand constraints, and revision process.
- Create the Drive folder and portal.
- Schedule the first source and delivery date.

### First seven days

- Deliver the first paid clip.
- Get it approved and posted.
- Record turnaround time, revision count, and client response.
- Ask one question: what would make the next clip more useful to the business?

### Monthly review

- Delivered versus promised.
- Approval turnaround.
- Posts published.
- Qualified comments, DMs, clicks, newsletter signups, or other BOF-relevant signals available to the client.
- Next month's topic and archive opportunities.
- Renewal and ascension decision.

## Ascension

Do not jump from $997 to an undefined $8,000 offer.

1. Weekly clips: $997 founding offer.
2. Twice-weekly clips: price after measured fulfillment cost; initial hypothesis $1,850.
3. Long-form pilot: one episode with fixed length, revision, thumbnail, and turnaround.
4. Long-form monthly: only after the pilot establishes labor and approval complexity.

Five long-form clients at $8,000 cannot be added to fifteen short-form clients without a production team. Capacity must precede ascension sales.

## Proof Without Personal Case Studies

Use third-party evidence as market context, not as a claim that Energy Dial will reproduce the result:

- Schneider Electric used localized video and executive thought-leader creative in a multi-touchpoint LinkedIn program; LinkedIn reports high completion and increased engagement.
- AGL's executive-led thought-leader campaign generated greater targeted reach and engagement than the executive's organic baseline.
- ACCIONA used 60-second native video to reach professional energy and infrastructure audiences; LinkedIn reports relevant reach and view-rate results.
- Shell's executive subject-matter publishing produced materially more reach and stakeholder engagement than the adviser's standalone blog.
- YouTube reports that multi-format publishing can connect short and long-form discovery, but its strongest published examples are music/creator cases and must not be presented as an energy-B2B guarantee.

The sales page should label these as platform or company case studies and state that results are not typical or promised.
